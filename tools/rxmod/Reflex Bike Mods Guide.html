Building bikes as .rxmod

From a Blender model and its PSD template to a bike in the game: how the bike rides as one of the game's own bikes, how to set the model up, the recipe, the export, the textures, mod.json, testing — with NFG COLE's 2025 Husqvarna FC 250 as the worked sample, start to finish.

blender_rxmod.py the Blender exporter: model -> .rxmesh, previews, report rxmod.py check, pack, list, extract, token (needs rxtrack.py beside it) psd_compose.py a PSD template's diffuse / spec / normal, without Photoshop sample-fc250/ the FC 250: .blend, recipe, maps, the mod, the .rxmod

The tools also live in the OpenReflex repo under tools/rxmod/; the full format spec is core/plan/RXMOD.md. The sample bike is NFG COLE's, shared with his permission for this kit.

Start here

A bike mod is one .rxmod file holding a whole new bike — its model and its textures — that rides as one of the game's own bikes. Players drop it into Mods/Bikes (any sub-folder) and each bike in it shows up in GARAGE > VEHICLE as a MOD card under the original it rides as, credited BY <author>.

1 · Blender

Model + recipe

Split the model into the parts that move, write a small JSON recipe, run blender_rxmod.py: out comes bike.rxmesh and a preview.

2 · Textures

PSD -> PNG

Export the diffuse (and spec) from the template — Photoshop or psd_compose.py. Powers of two, at most 4096.

3 · Pack

mod.json + check + pack

Name the original bike, the brand / model, the author and the materials; rxmod.py check, try the folder in the game, rxmod.py pack.

Everything here is tested against the game.The six NFG COLE bikes (12 bikes in all) went through exactly this pipeline: every one shows in the garage and race setup, rides and finishes a race with AI, and its suspension and steering move its parts. The FC 250 sample in this kit rebuilds byte for byte: its .blend + recipe.json give the same bike.rxmesh, and its mod/ folder packs to the same .rxmod players have.
Which builds read it..rxmod bikes load in the experimental OpenReflex builds (commit 466458c8 and later). The released 1.0.x line doesn't read them yet.

What a bike mod is

  • Its own look: your model and textures replace the original bike's model, plastics, frame, engine, bars, exhaust — everything (wheels optional, see below).
  • The original's physics, 1:1: the bike rides as an original (you name its VehicleModelId): engine, gearing, suspension, handling, mass, price and ownership are that bike's. Nothing in a .rxmod can change physics; the game's core simulates the original either way.
  • Animated by the original's rig: the game's pose of the original bike (steering, fork travel, swingarm, wheel spin, levers) moves your parts by the node each one is bound to.
  • Fitted at load: the game scales and aligns your model so its axles sit exactly on the original's, and your wheels to the original tyres' radius — no need to match the original's dimensions by hand.
  • Several bikes per file: a 250 and a 450 can share one mesh and one atlas (the archive stores equal bytes once).

The pipeline

bike.blend + recipe.json --blender_rxmod.py--> bike.rxmesh  (+ side.png preview, report.json)
template.psd             --psd_compose.py / Photoshop--> diffuse.png, spec.png
mymod/ mod.json + bike.rxmesh + diffuse.png + spec.png  --rxmod.py check--> ok  --(Mods/Bikes)--> try it
mymod/                   --rxmod.py pack--> My Bike.rxmod

Tools: Blender 4.x or 5.x (headless is fine), Python 3 (standard library) for rxmod.py, and numpy for psd_compose.py (Blender's own Python has it: /Applications/Blender.app/Contents/Resources/<version>/python/bin/python3.x).

Set up the model

  • Real size, any unit (the recipe's unit says metres per Blender unit: 1.0 in metres, 0.3048 for a model in feet, as MX Simulator / MX Bikes style models — all of NFG COLE's — are).
  • Standing on its wheels, Z up, nose along any horizontal axis (the exporter works out forward from the axles).
  • Split into the parts that move, as objects (or materials):
NodePut in itMoves withPreview colour
OBJ_Frameframe, engine, plastics, seat, tank, exhaust, footpegs, rear fender, radiatorsthe bikegrey
OBJ_UPR_Forkstriple clamps, upper fork tubes, bars, levers, grips, number plate, front fenderthe steeringblue
OBJ_LWR_Forksfork legs, front caliper, disc guardthe steering + fork travelcyan
OBJ_FRT_Tire / OBJ_BCK_Tiretyre, rim, spokes, hub, disc, sprocket (optional)the wheel's spin (+ travel)yellow / orange
OBJ_Swingarmswingarm, chain, chain guide, rear caliperthe rear suspensionmagenta
OBJ_Throttle, OBJ_ClutchLVR, OBJ_BrakeLVRoptional: the throttle tube and the leversthe rider's handsgreen
skinnedthe rear shock (and anything else that stretches)between two nodes (frame top, swingarm bottom)red
  • No wheels in the model? Fine — most of NFG COLE's bikes have none (MX Simulator models keep wheels apart). The game then draws the original bike's own tyres and rims on your axles. Give the exporter the axle centres ("axles"): an axle bolt modelled across the fork legs / swingarm is easy to find, its centre is the axle.
  • With wheels: bind them to OBJ_FRT_Tire / OBJ_BCK_Tire; the exporter takes their centres as the axles and the game scales them to the original tyre's radius (up to ±15 %).
  • Everything visible and set to render is exported. Hide reference images, spare parts and helper objects, or list what to include. Variant parts laid out beside the bike (pipes, bars) are moved into place with "offsets".
  • Around 100 000 triangles for the whole bike is fine (the NFG COLE bikes are 30k-160k).

The recipe

A JSON file telling the exporter which object is which part. Patterns are regular expressions (case-insensitive) on the object name (obj:) or the material name (mat:); the first rule that matches wins. The sample's, as used:

{
 "unit": 0.3048,                                       metres per Blender unit (the model is in feet)
 "include": ["^FMF FG$", "^ProTaper$", "^LowerFork$", "^Swingarm$"],     the objects that make this bike
 "offsets": { "FMF FG": [-9, 0, 0] },                  the FMF exhaust variant sits 9 units beside the bike: moved into place
 "axles":   { "front": [1.9118, 2.6211, -0.8947],      no wheels in the model: the axle bolts' centres (Blender coordinates)
              "rear":  [1.9118, -2.2629, -0.9409] },
 "nodes": [ ["obj:^ProTaper$",  "OBJ_UPR_Forks"],      bars, clamps, upper tubes, plate, fender
            ["obj:^LowerFork$", "OBJ_LWR_Forks"],
            ["obj:^Swingarm$",  "OBJ_Swingarm"],
            ["obj:.*",          "OBJ_Frame"] ],        everything else
 "skin": [ { "match": "mat:^Shock(\\.\\d+)?$", "top": "OBJ_Frame", "bottom": "OBJ_Swingarm" } ],
 "materials": [ ["mat:^(Plastics?|Seat|GasCap|BarPad|Dull Plastic|Plastic matte|Carbon Fiber|Rubber|Donuts|Clear|Material)(\\.\\d+)?$", "body"],
                ["mat:^Cast", "cast"],
                [".*", "metal"] ]                      Blender material -> the mod's material name in mod.json
}
KeyMeaning
unitMetres per Blender unit (default: the scene's unit scale).
include / excludeObject name patterns (default: every visible, rendered mesh).
offsets{object: [dx, dy, dz]} in Blender units: moves a variant laid out beside the bike into place.
nodes[[pattern, node], ...]: which node moves each object / material.
defaultThe node of what no rule names (default OBJ_Frame).
split + whereFor models merged into one mesh: "split": true matches each loose part on its own, and where rules place parts by position ({"node": "OBJ_FRT_Tire", "in_wheel": "front", "max_x": 0.09}, {"node": ..., "box": [[x,y,z],[x,y,z]]} in bike space, metres from the rear axle).
axlesThe axle centres in Blender coordinates, for a model without wheels (else the wheel parts' centres are used).
wheels{"front": pattern, "rear": pattern}: which parts' centres are the axles, when they aren't the OBJ_*_Tire parts.
steerOptional: two points on the steering axis (top, bottom), Blender coordinates. Default: fitted to the fork tubes.
pivotOptional: the swingarm pivot, Blender coordinates (default: the swingarm's front end).
skinParts stretched between two nodes: weighted along their long axis ("ramp": [0.35, 0.65] = where the blend happens).
materials[[pattern, mod material], ...]: several Blender materials may share one mod material (they share the atlas).

The kit's examples/ holds the recipes of all six NFG COLE bikes: models in feet and in metres, separate wheel objects (TM), one shared model for two bikes (KX), variants laid out side by side (YZ, CRF, FC, MC) and pivot / axle points given by hand (KX).

Export

blender -b bike.blend --python blender_rxmod.py -- --recipe recipe.json --out mymod/bike/bike.rxmesh \
        --report report.json --preview side.png
# textured instead of node colours (checks the UVs and the variant you picked):
blender -b bike.blend --python blender_rxmod.py -- --recipe recipe.json --out mymod/bike/bike.rxmesh \
        --preview three4.png --texture mymod/bike/diffuse.png --view three4

It prints blender_rxmod: bike.rxmesh: 9 surfaces, 81228 vertices, 102398 triangles, wheelbase 4.884 ft. A wheelbase far from 4.5-5 ft means a wrong unit or wrong axles. --view takes side, left, front, top or three4. Open the preview (the kit zip's copy of this guide shows the sample's).

report.json has the axles (feet, from the rear axle), the wheelbase, the steering axis and head, the swingarm pivot, every surface (node, material, skinned, counts) and which node each object went to — check it whenever a part moves wrong.

Opening downloaded .blend files.A .blend can carry Python scripts. Run Blender with auto-run off (-Y, --factory-startup) on files you didn't make; on macOS the NFG COLE files were also opened under sandbox-exec with no network and writes only to the output folder.

Textures

  • PNG or JPG, 8 bits, sides a power of two, at most 4096. NFG COLE's bikes: one 4096 x 4096 diffuse atlas and a 2048 x 2048 spec map each.
  • Exported the same way up as Blender maps them (the exporter writes Blender's UVs; the game handles the rest).
  • Colours are used as stored (no colour-space conversion).

From a PSD template without Photoshop (numpy needed: Blender's Python has it). Templates often carry a cover / "welcome" layer and a palette that must not end up on the bike:

python3 -I psd_compose.py layers "2025 FC 250 RE.psd"                       # the layer tree: find the cover / UV / palette layers
python3 -I psd_compose.py compose "2025 FC 250 RE.psd" diffuse.png --skip Welcome --skip "Color Palet" --skip "Color Pallet"
python3 -I psd_compose.py compose template.psd spec.png --only Spec --flatten 0,0,0        # a hidden Spec group alone
python3 -I psd_compose.py compose template.psd norm.png --only Norm --flatten 0.5,0.5,1    # a hidden Norm group alone
python3 -I psd_compose.py compose "8K template.psd" diffuse.png --size 4096                # an 8K template at 4K

It supports 8-bit RGB PSDs, groups (pass-through and isolated, with masks and opacity), layer and clipping masks and the common blend modes; adjustment layers and layer effects are not drawn (each skipped one is listed). Checked against Photoshop's own composite of a 4096 template: mean difference 0.24 / 255. The sample's fc/diffuse.png is the first command's output; its fc/spec.png is NFG COLE's 2025 FC 250 RE spec.png scaled to 2048.

FieldMeaning
format, version, type"reflex-mod", 1, "bikes".
id, bikes[].idOptional but recommended: stable names skins use to find your bike.

Materials

Each material borrows the look of one of the original bike's materials — its shader, lighting, reflections and mud — with your maps:

templateLooks likeUse it for
plasticthe graphics kit's plastics: glossy, reflectiveplastics, seat, number plates, tank, grips
framethe frame: painted metal, shine from the spec mapframe, swingarm, forks, bars, hardware, exhaust
enginethe engineengine cases, castings
wheelthe rimsrims, hubs, spokes
tirethe tyres (uses normal)tyres
chainthe chain: cut-outs from the diffuse's alphaanything with alpha cut-outs (with "alphaTest": true) — TM's wheels

Fields: diffuse, spec (greyscale: shine / reflection), normal (+ normalFlipY), sfx (an authored SFX map: R shine, G mud mask, B reflection), mud (0..1, default 1), shine (a scale of the spec), twoSided, alphaTest + alphaRef. Number plates and decals are part of your diffuse (a .rxmod bike has no run-time number).

Testing

  1. python3 rxmod.py check mymod ends with ok; its summary line per bike shows nodes, surfaces, triangles, the wheelbase and the materials.
  2. Copy the folder into <game>/Mods/Bikes/ (it loads unpacked: change and retry) and start an experimental build.
  3. GARAGE > VEHICLE > MY RIDES > MODDED BIKES: the card (FC 250 · HUSQVARNA · BY NFG COLE), turn the bike on the stage: wheels on the ground, no missing parts, no inside-out surfaces.
  4. Ride it (TEST RIDE or a race): steer at a standstill (the fork and bars turn together), land a jump (the forks and swingarm compress, the shock stretches), the wheels spin.
  5. python3 rxmod.py pack mymod "My Bike.rxmod" and swap the folder for the file.
Log lineMeaning
[RxMod] <file>: mod.json: ...The manifest doesn't read: the whole mod is left out.
[RxMod] ...: bike N: mesh ... is not in the modA mesh entry is missing.
the mod's axles are X ft apartUnits or axes wrong (a bike's axles are ~4.8 ft apart).
node X is not in <hierarchy>: drawn on YA node name the original doesn't have.
material X is not in mod.jsonThe mesh names a material mod.json lacks: drawn as plain plastic.
OBJ_FRT_Tire radius ... left as modelledThe wheel is more than 15 % off the original's tyre.

Online and skins

A rider's look carries the bike as "bike": "b:<content id>:<VehicleModelId>" (rxmod.py token; the sample's is b:bfde3bf08744:18). Riders with the same .rxmod see your bike, anyone else the original bike it rides as; physics never change. Any byte change is a new version the others need too.

Skins (liveries) for your bike are separate small .rxmod files that name your mod's id and bike id and replace your map files — that is what the id fields are for, and why the atlas paths in your mod.json should stay stable between versions. See the skin kit (rxmod-skin-kit).

The .rxmesh format

For exporters from other tools (3ds Max, Maya, a converter): what blender_rxmod.py writes. Little-endian; str = u16 byte length + UTF-8.

"RXMS"  u32 version = 1  u32 flags = 0
u32 nodes;      each: str name, i32 parent (-1 root, else an earlier node), f32[16] world matrix (row-major,
                column vectors: the translation is elements 3, 7, 11)
u32 materials;  each: str name (a key of the bike's "materials" in mod.json)
u32 surfaces;   each: u32 material, u32 node, u32 flags (1 skinned, 2 uv1, 4 tangents), u32 vertices V, u32 indices I,
                f32[3V] positions, f32[3V] normals, f32[2V] uv0, [f32[2V] uv1], [f32[4V] tangents],
                [u8[4V] bone nodes (255 = none), f32[4V] weights], u32[I] triangle list
  • Space: feet, X right, Y up, Z forward (left-handed), any origin. Triangles clockwise seen from outside (Blender's counter-clockwise faces are reversed when the axes swap). UVs as the modelling tool has them (origin bottom-left).
  • Nodes named as the original's (table above) at your pivots: OBJ_Frame root; OBJ_UPR_Forks and OBJ_LWR_Forks on the steering axis with their Y axis along it; OBJ_FRT_Tire / OBJ_BCK_Tire at the axles (required); OBJ_Swingarm at the pivot.
  • A rigid surface moves with its node; a skinned one blends up to four nodes per vertex.
  • rxmod.py check reads a .rxmesh fully (counts, bounds, node names, the wheelbase) — use it on your exporter's output.

The container

A .rxmod is an RXAR container exactly like a .rxtrack (see the rxtrack kit's guide, or core/plan/RXARCHIVE.md) with the header kind MODS (0x53444F4D), mod.json at the root, the meshes and pictures at the paths it names, and the index metadata {"format":"reflex-mod","type":"bikes",...}. rxmod.py pack writes it (entries stored, a SHA-256 each, equal bytes stored once); rxmod.py list / extract read one.

Rules checklist

  • Every bike names its original's VehicleModelId, same class (and brand where the game has it).
  • The mesh has OBJ_FRT_Tire and OBJ_BCK_Tire (the axles); the wheelbase reads ~4.5 - 5 ft.
  • Every material the mesh uses is in mod.json, with a template.
  • Pictures: PNG / JPG, powers of two, at most 4096.
  • author set; id and bike ids set if skins should find you.
  • rxmod.py check says ok; the card shows; the bike rides, steers and compresses right.
  • Only your own art, or art you have the author's permission to ship.
  • No physics: power, handling and price are the original's, by design.
  • No game files in a mod.

FAQ

My model has no wheels. Do I need to make some?

No: give the axles and the game draws the original bike's tyres and rims (as on every NFG COLE bike but the TM).

Can a 250 and a 450 share a model?

Yes: two entries in bikes, the same mesh and maps, different bike numbers (the CRF, KX, MC and FC do this).

The forks swing sideways when I steer.

Your steering axis is far from the original's: check steer_axis / steer_head_ft in the report, give "steer" two points on your head stem's axis, and make sure no frame part ended up on OBJ_UPR_Forks.

A part stays still / moves with the wrong thing.

Look it up in report.json's objects (object -> node) and the node preview; fix its nodes rule (first match wins: put specific rules before obj:.*).
